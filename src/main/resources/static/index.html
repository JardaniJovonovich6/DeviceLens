Why these readings matter

You do not need to fill in a form for a website to learn something about your browser.

Language, timezone, display size and browser version help websites work on your device. The same signals can also be combined to compare visits or make assumptions about a visitor. A single reading says little; a collection can reveal more than you expect.

DeviceLens makes those readings visible so you can ask better questions: Does this site need that information? Who will receive it? How long will it be kept? We show the observations and their limits, without claiming that they identify you or prove you are anonymous.

A small example

Language and timezone may suggest a broad region. They do not prove where you live. Memory and processor counts may suggest a device class; they do not establish its exact model. Cookies store data in a browser, while fingerprinting compares observable signals—clearing cookies alone does not necessarily stop that comparison.

What this inspection cannot tell you

Private hardware identifiers
IMEI, serial number, MAC address and your phone number are not exposed by the browser APIs used here.
Location, camera or microphone content
We query permission states only. We do not request coordinates, capture media, enumerate devices, or inspect your files.
Your identity, VPN or private-browsing mode
These readings cannot reliably establish your real name, VPN use, private mode, tracking-protection settings, or exact retail device model.
Whether a website stores your IP
A server sees the connection’s public IP when serving a page. DeviceLens does not look it up or display it, and application access logging is disabled. The hosting provider can retain infrastructure logs.

Questions about the report

Why is my exact device model unavailable?

Many browsers do not expose a model, especially desktop browsers and Safari. Android Client Hints sometimes provide a model code. A generic “iPhone” family or a model code is not a verified retail name. Unknown values stay unavailable.

Does an unavailable value mean I am anonymous?

No. It may reflect missing browser support, a denied query or reduced data. Login accounts, public IP addresses and ordinary request headers can still connect activity.

Are these accurate hardware specifications?

They are browser-reported values or labeled inferences. Memory and connection estimates are approximate. Screen size uses CSS pixels, which can differ from physical pixels. User-Agent strings and Client Hints can be reduced or spoofed.

What happens when I copy, download or contact you?

Copy writes a report to your clipboard after you click. Download saves JSON on your device. The contact link opens your email app with a subject line; it does not attach a report or send a message automatically. Review any information you choose to include yourself.

Does a VPN or private window hide these values?

A VPN can change the public IP a website sees. Private browsing mainly limits local history and session storage. Neither automatically hides all browser-visible signals.

How can I check that reports stay local?

Open your browser’s Network developer tools, reload this page and inspect the requests. You should see this site’s page and static assets, with no report POSTs or third-party asset requests. The site’s Content Security Policy blocks script connections. Ordinary page requests still reach the host.
